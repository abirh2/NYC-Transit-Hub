## Design Specificity Verdict

Transit-specific content sits inside a category-interchangeable dashboard shell.
MTA bullets and live copy are authentic, but the title plus equal-card grid could
belong to finance, fitness, or infrastructure monitoring. The missing product
idea is the rider's immediate decision: what to catch next and whether their own
trip is affected.

The deterministic detector returned zero findings for `app/page.tsx`. This is a
scope caveat rather than proof of a strong surface because that file is a thin
composition wrapper and the hierarchy problems live across imported cards.
Mutable browser injection was unavailable, so fresh-tab accessibility and visual
inspection were used; no user-visible overlay was produced.

## Overall Impression

The page is technically credible and visually coherent but behaves as a feature
directory. Its biggest opportunity is to replace the first empty/setup card and
global summaries with one compact, personalized transit decision surface.

## What's Working

- Authentic route bullets, severity language, destinations, and live metrics.
- Solid loading, empty, error, and responsive foundations.
- Persistent labeled navigation, mobile thumb-zone navigation, and semantic
  route labels alongside color.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] No singular rider decision.** Replace the feature grid's lead with
   nearby next departures, personal service context, and a clear planning action.
2. **[P1] Empty station setup dominates.** Use useful nearby or saved content
   first and reduce setup to a compact affordance.
3. **[P1] “System Status” contradicts alerts.** Separate developer feed health
   from rider-facing route condition; remove feed health from Home.
4. **[P2] Uniform cards flatten urgency.** Establish primary rider, compact
   disruption/status, and quiet analytics levels.
5. **[P2] Inconsistent card interactions.** Standardize direct links, focus,
   semantics, and heading structure.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex:** must scan seven modules before reaching a specific route or trip.
- **Sam:** card titles are paragraphs, and similar cards expose inconsistent
  keyboard/link behavior.
- **Casey:** a long mobile card stack and many independent requests delay the
  one-handed next-transit decision.

## Minor Observations

- “Your Station” and “home station” should be inclusive “saved transit” language.
- Alerts and incidents overlap without explaining their distinction.
- Reliability values lack a unit or definition.
- “All feeds operational” is developer reassurance, not rider service context.

## Questions to Consider

- If Home answered only one question, should it be “What should I take next?”
- Which global metrics change a rider's next five-minute decision?
- Should any alert be above the fold when it affects none of the rider's routes?
